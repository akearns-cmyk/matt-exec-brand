## Core Philosophy

Build genuine thought leadership in workforce compliance, heavy industry, and the future of work. MyPass appears as context, not the subject.

Posts feel like Matt wrote them with a strong opinion and a clear head — not marketing copy.

**From Kieran Flanagan:** If a post doesn't give the reader a new lens, it's just a better-written version of the news. The goal is a mental model they can steal in 30 seconds — not tips, frameworks.

**From Jake Ward:** Post content that's been live-tested. Specific, data-backed observations from real situations outperform polished opinion every time. Transparency about the messy middle builds more credibility than the clean result.

## 2026 Algorithm Reality

The LinkedIn algorithm shifted from Social Graph (who you know) to Interest Graph (what interests you). This changes how posts get distributed.

### What the algorithm rewards

- **Dwell time** — posts with 61+ seconds of dwell time achieve 15x more engagement than posts that get a 3-second scroll-past. Structure and substance drive this, not tricks.
- **Comments** — carry approximately 15x more weight than likes. Three thoughtful replies outperforms 50 quick reactions.
- **Topic DNA alignment** — the algorithm reads Matt's profile and post, checks if they match. Consistent posting in your niche builds compounding authority. Going off-topic suppresses reach.
- **The Golden Hour** — the first 60 minutes after posting are make-or-break. The algorithm shows the post to 2–5% of the network, measures signals, then decides whether to expand.
- **Saves** — signal lasting value and trigger resurfacing days later. Framework posts and reference-style content get saved. Generic opinions do not.

### What kills reach

- External links in post body (up to 60% reach penalty — put links in comments if needed)
- Generic hashtags (#Leadership, #Success) — confuse topic classification
- Engagement bait ("Agree?" posts) — flagged as low-value
- Going off the established topic niche — profile-content misalignment is penalised
- Posting twice within 18–24 hours — kills the earlier post's reach window

**Practical implication:** One post per week, timed well, on a specific topic, structured to earn dwell time and prompt real comments — beats five posts that get scrolled past.

## Post Structure — The Anatomy of a Winning Post

### 1. Hook (Lines 1–2) — Most Important Real Estate

The only job of the hook is to earn the "see more" click.

**Option A — Provocative / counterintuitive (Flanagan-style — opens like someone who's already decided):**
> "The compliance software market is broken. And most operators don't realise they're the ones paying for it."
> "Some of the safest worksites I've visited have the worst digital compliance records."
> "Everyone's automating onboarding. Almost no-one's automating what happens when something goes wrong."

**Option B — Grounded / specific (Ward-style — starts with a real moment or number):**
> "Had an interesting conversation in Houston last week that I keep thinking about."
> "We lost a $400K deal last year because of a data silo problem we didn't even know we had."
> "120,000 workers. 14 different onboarding platforms. One client. Still somehow a compliance gap."

**Never open with:** "I'm excited to announce..." / "Thrilled to share..." / "Following up on..." / "I" as the first word.

---

### 2. Body — Four Formats

Choose ONE format per post. Mixing formats reduces dwell time.

---

#### FORMAT 1: Plain-Text Insight Post
*Best for: industry takes, observations, strong opinions*

The workhorse. One sharp observation, unpacked with specifics.

**Structure:**
1. Hook
2. Context or observation that sets up the tension (2–3 short paragraphs)
3. The counterintuitive element or key insight
4. The implication — what this means for the reader
5. Close (question, punchy statement, or low-key invite to discuss)

**Rules:**
- 1–3 sentences per paragraph, generous line breaks
- Build progressively — don't front-load the punchline
- MyPass as supporting evidence, never the subject
- Specific over general: "120,000 active workers" beats "large workforce"
- **Target length: 150–250 words**

---

#### FORMAT 2: Simple Framework Post
*Best for: concept explainers, mental models, decision tools*

Flanagan's signature move. Give the reader a mental model they can steal and apply. These get saved. Saves are algorithm gold.

**Structure:**
1. Hook: name the problem or gap the framework solves
2. Brief context (1–2 lines): why this matters right now
3. The framework: 3–5 clean points, each 1–2 lines
4. Apply it back: one line showing how this maps to heavy industry
5. Close: invite them to apply it or ask which step they'd add

**Format rules:**
- Each point starts with a label
- One clear takeaway per point — don't double up
- No jargon in the labels — the reader should understand immediately

**Example structure:**
```
[Hook — name the problem]

Here's how I think about it:

1. [Label]: [1-sentence explanation]
2. [Label]: [1-sentence explanation]
3. [Label]: [1-sentence explanation]
4. [Label]: [1-sentence explanation]

[Apply to your context — 1-2 lines]

[Close]
```

**Target length: 150–300 words**

---

#### FORMAT 3: Problem-Reframing Take
*Best for: challenging received wisdom, generating comments*

The most comment-generating format. Take something everyone "knows" and show why the framing is wrong. Flanagan: "giving the reader a new lens."

**The test:** does this post make the reader look at something differently, or just add another opinion to the pile?

**Structure:**
1. Hook: name the conventional belief
2. "Here's what's actually happening" — the reframe
3. Why the conventional framing leads people wrong (be specific)
4. What the correct framing implies for how to act
5. Close: challenge or genuine question

**Contrast technique (Flanagan):** frame as Old vs New, Appearance vs Reality, or What Everyone Does vs What Actually Works. Contrast creates cognitive tension that earns dwell time and prompts comments.

**Target length: 150–250 words**

---

#### FORMAT 4: Simple Conceptual Diagram
*Best for: ideas with visual structure — phases, spectrums, 2x2 trade-offs, before/after states*

Carousels outperform plain text for dwell time significantly — each swipe signals engagement to the algorithm.

**For a text-based diagram post:**
- Introduce the concept in the opening
- Lay out the diagram visually using plain text, dashes, and arrows
- Annotate each element in 1–2 lines
- Close with the key insight the diagram reveals

**For a carousel (PDF):**
- 5–10 slides, one clear takeaway per slide
- First slide = the hook (same rules as above)
- Last slide = the most valuable insight — give people a reason to swipe through
- Each slide short, visual, not text-heavy
- Saves drive algorithm distribution for days

---

### 3. Close

Rotate through these — do not default to a question every time:

- **Direct question** (use sparingly — max 2 per month): "What are you seeing on your end?"
- **Punchy statement that lands the point:** no question, just the take
- **Low-key invite to connect:** "Happy to share more if useful"
- **Soft challenge:** "Most people do the opposite — curious if you've found the same"
- **Flanagan-style PS or aside:** adds texture without being promotional

**Avoid:** ad-like CTAs, "drop a comment below," manufactured urgency, "Thoughts?"

---

### 4. Hashtags

2–4 at end, own line. Niche and industry-specific only — generic tags actively hurt topic classification in 2026.

**Good:** #WorkforceCompliance #ContractorManagement #HeavyIndustry #MyPassGlobal #EnergyIndustry #MiningIndustry #OperationalExcellence

**Avoid:** #Leadership #Success #FutureOfWork #Innovation — too generic, wrong audience signal

## Voice & Tone

- **Confident, not arrogant** — earned opinions, stated directly, with room for dialogue
- **Operator talking sideways (Flanagan)** — peer-to-peer, not talking down or talking vague. Like you're in the same Slack channel trying to solve a real problem
- **Specific over general (Ward)** — numbers, names, situations. "We ran this live and here's what happened" beats polished theory
- **Self-deprecating when genuine** — a deal that didn't go as expected, being outrun by your own team — these are assets, not liabilities
- **Intellectually curious** — genuine questions, not rhetorical ones
- **No buzzwords:** game-changer, disrupting, revolutionising, journey, passion, thought leader, synergy, ecosystem, unlock, leverage

## What to Write About

**Industry takes / problem-reframing:**
- Workforce compliance trends and where the industry is getting it wrong
- The gap between compliance theatre and genuine safety outcomes
- How heavy industry is (or isn't) adapting to labour market shifts
- Observations from client conversations that reveal a broader pattern
- Real cost of contractor onboarding friction (use specific numbers when available)
- Received wisdom in the industry that's actually wrong — and why

**Framework posts:**
- Apply an idea from another field to workforce management (target quality bar: Jevons' Paradox applied to compliance tech)
- Commonly misunderstood aspects of contractor management
- What "digital transformation" actually means on a drill site
- A decision model Matt uses that others could steal

**Event recaps:**
- Warm and specific — name people, tag them
- Don't recap the agenda — share the insight or moment that stayed with you
- One concrete takeaway beats three generic ones

**Team / culture:**
- MyPass team wins, client milestones, moments showing the company's character
- Connect individual stories to broader mission
- Authentic over polished

**Personal candour:**
- Moments revealing what Matt actually stands for
- Self-deprecating observations from travel, sport, daily life
- Use when it's real, not manufactured

## What to Avoid

- Don't post just to post — one strong post per week beats five forgettable ones
- Don't end every post with a question — mix closers
- Don't let MyPass become the subject
- Don't use the post to sell
- Don't write the same post twice with different words
- Don't put external links in the post body — they kill reach
- Don't use generic hashtags (#Leadership, #Innovation) — they confuse topic classification
- Don't post twice within 18 hours — you'll cannibalise your own reach window

## Algorithm-Smart Habits (2026)

- **Post timing:** AEST mornings (7–9am) or early afternoon (12–2pm) — catches Australia awake and Asia/EU starting
- **The Golden Hour:** In the 60 minutes after posting, respond to every comment, even briefly. This signals engagement velocity and extends distribution.
- **Engage before and after posting:** Comment substantively on 2–3 relevant posts before and after publishing. Signals active community membership, not just broadcasting.
- **Revive a dying post:** 24 hours after posting, reply to an older comment or tag a relevant expert. Can trigger a second distribution wave.
- **Profile-content alignment:** Matt's headline and About section should use the same language as his posts. Keyword alignment between profile and posts is a distribution signal.
