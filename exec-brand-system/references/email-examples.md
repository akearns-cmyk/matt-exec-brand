## How to Use This File

1. Identify the scenario type (referral response, re-engagement, post-discovery, partner, internal, etc.)

---

## Signoff Reference

| Signoff | Context |
|---|---|
| `Kind regards,` | New/formal external contact, prospect |
| `Regards,` | Multi-stakeholder external, slightly formal |
| `Regs,` | External contact after prior interaction, relationship warming |
| `Cheers,` | Trusted external partner |
| *(none)* | Internal action email — no signoff needed |

## Example 1: Referral Response

**Context:** Responding to an introduction from David to Adelina (external prospect)
**Register:** UTG trust-based — new prospect via warm referral

```
Thanks for the introduction David. Moving you to bcc to spare your inbox.

Hi Adelina,

I'd welcome the opportunity for a brief introductory call if you're open to it?

I'd like to understand more about your current solution and how well that is working for you.

As David mentioned, we were unsuccessful in the COIN selection process, however we've
been working with Alcoa for several years as the system of record for the Verisafe VoC's
(MyPass is the technology partner for the Chamber of Minerals & Energy's Verisafe group
which includes Chevron, Shell, Rio Tinto, BHP and Woodside. All of these members use
MyPass beyond Verisafe with the exception of RioTinto and Alcoa).

For your interest, Elsabe Muller was a key stakeholder in BHP's Integrated Contractor
Management initiative which resulted in MyPass getting rolled out in Chile and across
Nickel West. We're in scoping mode for the rest of the Australian assets.

Happy to share more over a call.

Kind regards,
```

**Key patterns:**
- Move referrer to BCC immediately, acknowledge it in the first line
- Lead with curiosity about their current solution (UTG — not MyPass capabilities)
- Honest about competitive loss — doesn't hide it, uses it as credibility
- Named individuals and specific programs as proof points
- Light, no-pressure close
- `Kind regards,` signoff for new prospect context

## Example 2: Re-engagement After Going Quiet

**Context:** Following up with Adelina who hadn't responded
**Register:** UTG trust-based — re-engagement, prior contact established

```
Hi Adelina,

Just looping back to see if you're open to a brief introductory call?

I'll share a further update on the Verisafe program that we're working together on.

Have a great weekend.

Regs,
```

**Key patterns:**
- Extremely short — two sentences of substance
- No pressure, no guilt about not responding
- Specific hook (Verisafe update) — not generic "just checking in"
- Warm personal close ("Have a great weekend")
- `Regs,` signoff — relationship has warmed since first contact
- Does not open with "follow up" or any variation

## Example 3: Post-Discovery Call Follow-up

**Context:** To Vale team after a productive discovery session
**Register:** External client — warm/collaborative, multi-stakeholder

```
Hi team,

Thanks for Friday's productive discussion (hi Paulo - hope you're doing well).

The meeting reinforced that there's a strong alignment between Vale's current challenges
and how similar organizations have successfully deployed MyPass.

I've attached the meeting summary, with my presentation available here.

Action Required: To prepare a pilot proposal as part of our Joint Impact Plan, please
review the attached spreadsheet and complete Column E. If possible, please send this back
by Wednesday so we can maintain momentum. Your responses will enable me to develop a
targeted Joint Impact Plan that addresses Vale's specific requirements.

Additionally, I have three questions:

1. Reference conversations - Would speaking with someone from Methanex Canada or BHP's
Technology Team (who deployed our solution in Chile) help assess MyPass's fit for Vale?

2. Budget parameters - What funding constraints should we work within for the contractor
onboarding, compliance and demobilisation solution?

3. Decision-making process - Who within Vale Base Metals will make the final go/no-go
decision following the roadshow?

4. Timeline - given the gaps in your Canadian operations, is there a specific timeline
Vale is working towards to get a solution in place?

Looking forward to your feedback.

Regards,
```

**Key patterns:**
- Personal aside embedded naturally mid-sentence ("hi Paulo - hope you're doing well")
- "Action Required" as a bold label — makes the ask unmissable in a multi-stakeholder email
- Numbered questions with labels, not bullets — each has a category name and a specific question
- "Maintain momentum" — characteristic Matt language
- Uses client's framing (Joint Impact Plan)
- `Regards,` signoff for slightly formal multi-stakeholder context

## Example 4: External Partner — Candid and Direct

**Context:** Response to Abe about a BD partnership opportunity Matt isn't ready for
**Register:** Candid/direct — trusted external partner

```
Thanks for coming back on this. My gut hasn't changed - the whole situation feels messy.

I'm going to leave it for now. Once BHP gets some momentum in the next few months,
I'm happy to revisit whether Chris or Brandon could play a BD role at that point.

But even then, I would need convincing on the longevity of Chris' clients, as my
experience was that they were very loyal to Chris and his Bayardo training business.
Ie he has massive influence over whether they buy, or whether they cancel.

Regardless, right now my attention needs to stay on the work we're delivering here
and our Woodside + Wood rollouts.

Appreciate you keeping me across it and thinking of me. Just let him know that I
don't have the bandwidth right now.

Let's stay in touch.

Cheers,
```

**Key patterns:**
- "My gut hasn't changed - the whole situation feels messy" — candour only available in trusted relationships
- No hedging on the decision — clear and direct
- Explains the reasoning (client loyalty dynamic) — treats partner as intelligent
- Protective of bandwidth without over-apologising
- "Ie" (not "i.e.") — casual spelling, authentic voice
- Warm close despite delivering a no
- `Cheers,` signoff for trusted partner

## Example 5: Internal Action Email

**Context:** To Stuart about NZ client retention
**Register:** Internal — punchy/action-tagged, no niceties

```
Stu- as discussed. Am exploring this as part of our NZ client retention play
(Methanex, OMV + Beach). Plus to try and get a couple of others along (Todd, Genesis etc).

As a first step I'm just reaching out the MD of Methanex and my contact at OMV to gauge interest.
```

**Key patterns:**
- No greeting — just "Stu-" with a dash
- "As discussed" signals context already shared verbally; no re-explanation needed
- Named clients in brackets — specific, not generic
- Clear first step stated
- No signoff
- Two sentences total — ruthlessly short

## Example 6: External Sales Re-engagement (Context-Rich)

**Context:** Reaching out to Ryan after a conversation with Zoe Chesters at Mayfair
**Register:** UTG trust-based — new contact via internal referral, relationship context established

```
Hi Ryan,

I'd like to reconnect given that things have evolved with the Mayfair acquisition.

Stuart and I had a good chat with Zoe Chesters this morning about the NAO system
requirements. She flagged you as the right person to speak with on the training and
LMS side of things, and I want to make sure I understand your requirements before
we go much further.

From our conversation with Zoe, the core challenge is the disconnect between the
training system (SuccessFactors), gate access (Gallagher), and the manual processes
in between - which sounds like it sits across both your world and hers. As you're
aware, we've been running the turnaround onboarding for Dyno Nobel for about five
years, so we're not starting from scratch with the context, but we'd want to
understand what the LMS and training requirements look like specifically for NAO going forward.

Would you have 30 minutes in the next couple of weeks to walk Stuart and me through
your side of it? Happy to work around your schedule.

Kind regards,
Matt Smith
CEO, MyPass Global
[msmith@mypassglobal.com]
```

**Key patterns:**
- Specific trigger stated upfront ("Mayfair acquisition" — the event that justifies re-contact)
- Names the person who made the referral (Zoe Chesters)
- Summarises the problem as Matt understood it — shows he listened and has domain knowledge
- Specific systems named (SuccessFactors, Gallagher) — not generic "your current systems"
- Proof point integrated naturally ("Dyno Nobel for about five years")
- Collaborative framing: "before we go much further" / "understand your requirements"
- Flexible ask: "30 minutes in the next couple of weeks... Happy to work around your schedule"
- Full signature block for new/formal external contact
- `Kind regards,` signoff

## Example 7: External Email with Internal PS

**Context:** Email to Ken (external) with a PS that pivots to Matt's own team
**Register:** Main body — external partner; PS — internal aside

```
[Main email to Ken about call timing and agenda...]

PS- for my folks, you can check out some of Ken's outputs below - he has some
solid angles that we can explore. Example [here], plus attached.
```

**Key patterns:**
- PS used to deliberately switch register mid-email (external → internal aside)
- "for my folks" — casual, inclusive internal language
- Signals something to Matt's team without making Ken feel talked around — Ken can see it, it's transparent
- This is a deliberate structural device, not accidental
- Use PS this way when Matt needs to address two audiences in one send

## Extracted Patterns

Patterns that appear across multiple examples — reliable signals of authentic Matt voice:

**Opening moves:**
- Referral: acknowledge the referrer, move to BCC, state curiosity about their situation
- Re-engagement: extremely short, specific hook, no guilt language
- Post-discovery: personal aside embedded naturally, "Action Required" for clear asks
- Partner/candid: lead with gut read, explain reasoning, warm close despite the no
- Internal: no greeting, "as discussed," named specifics, no signoff

**Characteristic phrases and constructions:**
- "My gut hasn't changed"
- "Maintain momentum"
- "Happy to share more / work around your schedule"
- "Before we go much further"
- "Just looping back" (re-engagement only — not a generic opener)
- Named individuals as proof points (never anonymous "a major client")
- Specific systems, programs, and initiatives named
- "Ie" (not "i.e.")
- Brackets for asides: "(hi Paulo - hope you're doing well)"

**What never appears in any example:**
- "Following up" or "Just following up" as an opener
- "I hope this finds you well"
- Generic social proof without names
- Apology for being in touch
- More than one ask per email (except post-discovery numbered questions)
- Exclamation points in external emails
