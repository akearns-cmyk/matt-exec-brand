# CEO Weekly Email — Format Reference

> This file defines the structure, tone, and rules for Matt's internal CEO Weekly Update email.
> Load this file when drafting the weekly internal update.
> Target length: 600–900 words total.

---

## Purpose

The CEO Weekly Update is Matt's primary internal communication channel. It:
- Creates alignment across the leadership team on what matters this week
- Signals what Matt is paying attention to and why
- Recognises specific people and contributions by name
- Sets the tone for how the company talks to itself

It is NOT a status report. It is not a list of what happened. It is Matt's curated perspective on what the week meant.

---

## Audience

**Primary:** MyPass leadership team
**Secondary (if forwarded):** Senior managers and team leads

**Tone:** Direct, warm, honest. The internal register — not sales mode, not performance. Matt speaking to people he trusts.

---

## Structure

### Section 1 — Opening Frame (50–100 words)
One paragraph. Sets the lens for the week — not a summary of events, but a frame for how to think about them.

Examples of what this is NOT:
- "This week was a big one for the team."
- "We had a lot going on this week."

Examples of what this IS:
- A tension Matt noticed and is sitting with
- A pattern that showed up across multiple conversations
- A signal from the market, a customer, or the team that deserves attention

---

### Section 2 — The Week's Signal Moments (200–300 words)
The 2–4 things that actually mattered this week. Not everything that happened — the things worth naming.

For each signal moment:
- What happened (specific, not general)
- Why it matters (the implication, not just the fact)
- Who was involved (name them if recognition is warranted)

**Format:** Short paragraphs, not bullets. Bullets flatten; paragraphs carry weight.

---

### Section 3 — What We're Watching (100–150 words)
The 1–3 things on Matt's radar that the team should also be watching.

This is not task assignment — it is shared situational awareness. Things like:
- A customer situation that's developing
- A competitive or regulatory signal
- An internal dynamic that needs collective attention

---

### Section 4 — Recognition (100–150 words)
Name specific people. Name specific contributions. Not generic praise.

**Do:** "[Name] turned around the [specific deliverable] under a tight deadline and the quality was excellent."
**Do not:** "The team has been working really hard and I'm grateful for everyone's effort."

If there's nothing specific worth recognising this week, say nothing rather than fabricate generic praise.

---

### Section 5 — The One Thing (50–80 words)
One question, challenge, or frame for the week ahead. Not a list of priorities — one thing Matt wants the team to carry into next week.

Can be:
- A question worth sitting with
- A principle to apply to current work
- A challenge to the default way of doing something

---

### Sign-off
Matt's voice. Conversational. Not a template.
Typically: brief, warm, and specific to the moment — not a generic closer.

---

## Tone Rules — Internal Register

- Honest before polished
- Direct before diplomatic (without being harsh)
- Recognition is specific or it's omitted
- Candour about what's hard is more valuable than false confidence
- Matt can say "I don't know yet" or "this is still messy" — that's a feature, not a bug

---

## What This Email Is NOT

- Not a board update (different audience, different register)
- Not a performance review in disguise
- Not a vehicle for announcements that should be in Slack or a meeting
- Not an opportunity to address interpersonal issues (those go elsewhere)
