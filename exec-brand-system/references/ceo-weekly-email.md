# CEO Weekly Email — Format Reference

> This file defines the exact structure, tone, and cultural vocabulary for Matt's internal CEO Weekly Update.
> Load this file when drafting the weekly internal update.
> Do NOT invent Good News items or priority status updates — these must come from Matt.

---

## Overview

The CEO Weekly Update is a narrative-led weekly round-up sent to the MyPass team. It has a distinct structure, tone, and cultural vocabulary that differs from all other communication types. This is not a standard email — it is a recognition and alignment tool with structured priority tracking.

**Target length:** 400–700 words (excluding the priority table)

---

## Full Format Structure

### 1. Header Block

Table format at the top of the email:

```
WEEKLY ROUND UP │ [DAY] [DATE ORDINAL] [MONTH] [YEAR]
```

Example: `WEEKLY ROUND UP │ Friday 9th May 2026`

---

### 2. Opening Paragraph (Narrative / Personal)

- Brief personal note — where Matt has been, what he's returning from, what he's noticing
- Warm but purposeful — sets the energy for the week
- Transitions into 1–2 specific company highlights or things he's seen or heard
- 2–4 sentences maximum— conversational tone
- This is the one place in Matt's communications where personal context (travel, weekend, a moment he noticed) belongs

**Example:**
> "I'm back from my birthday long weekend and feeling refreshed after some cold adventure in Queenstown NZ (I feel like this photo sums it up!)"

---

### 3. ✨ Good News Section

**Header:** `# ✨ Good News`

This is the recognition engine of the email. Each item:
- Opens with a **bold values label** (see Values Language below)
- Names specific individuals — always plural where a team contributed
- Describes the specific thing they did, not just that they did well
- Connects to broader business impact or significance
- Sometimes draws a link to what it means for clients or prospects

**Typical length:** 3–6 Good News items per week

**Example item format:**
> **Walk the Walk + Empower Our Customers**
> [Name] and [Name] turned around the [specific deliverable] for [client/situation] under a compressed timeline. The client noted it directly in their follow-up. This is exactly the standard we're building toward for enterprise onboarding.

---

### 4. 🎯 Q[N] Priorities Section

**Header:** `# 🎯 Q[N] Priorities ([Month] – [Month] [Year])`

Table format:

| # | Priority | Owner | Status | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Priority description] | [Initials] | ● GREEN | [1–2 sentence update] |
| 2 | [Priority description] | [Initials] | ● AMBER | [1–2 sentence update] |
| 3 | [Priority description] | [Initials] | ● RED | [1–2 sentence update] |

**Status indicators:**
- ● GREEN — on track
- ● AMBER — at risk, needs attention
- ● RED — off track, escalation needed

Comments are direct and factual. No softening language around RED items.

---

### 5. 🌟 Looking Ahead Section

**Header:** `# 🌟 Looking Ahead`

- 2–4 sentences
- Forward-focused energy
- Connects the current week's work to the quarter priority
- Closes with a motivating but not hollow call to action

**Example:**
> "The WAIO pilot is our north star this quarter. Every conversation, every delivery, every internal process improvement is pointing toward it. Every week from here matters, and we'll need all hands on deck to get there in shape."

---

### 6. Sign-off

```
Have a productive week.
Matt
```

Fixed. Does not vary. No alternatives.

---

## Company Values Language

These labels appear in **bold** in the Good News section. Use them accurately — they are specific, not interchangeable.

| Value | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Walk the Walk** | Delivering on commitments, owning outcomes |
| **Challenge the Norm** | Questioning assumptions, finding better approaches |
| **Bring Out the Best** | Developing people, creating conditions for others to excel |
| **Empower Our Customers** | Going above and beyond for client outcomes |

Items can carry multiple values labels: e.g., **Walk the Walk + Challenge the Norm**

---

## Tone Notes

- More personal than all other Matt communications — he shares where he's been, what he's noticed
- Recognition is specific and named — never generic ("great job to the team" is not acceptable)
- Individual contributions connect to company-level impact
- Emoji section headers (✨, 🎯, 🌟) are standard — this is the one context in all of Matt's communications where emojis are part of the format
- Priority table language is direct and factual
- Looking Ahead carries genuine energy — not hollow motivation

---

## What to Ask Before Drafting

Do not draft a CEO Weekly without this information from Matt:

1. **Good News stories** — who, what they did specifically, what the impact was
2. **Q priority status changes** — any changes since last week, any new comments
3. **Looking Ahead focus** — what's the key theme or north star for next week
4. **Personal opening note** — where Matt has been or what he's noticing this week

If any of these are missing, ask before drafting. Do not fill gaps with invented content.
