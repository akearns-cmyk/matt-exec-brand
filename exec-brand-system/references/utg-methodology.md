# UTG Trust-Based Selling — Methodology Reference

> Load this file for ALL prospect-facing communications: cold outreach, follow-ups, re-engagement, objection handling, commercial discussions.
> Do NOT apply UTG register to internal comms, existing client comms, or established partner comms — see Register Map in `foundation/creator-style.md`.

---

## Core Philosophy

UTG governs all prospect-facing communications. The goal is genuine fit discovery, not sales pressure.

**Three principles:**
1. **Diffuse pressure** — establish if there's a good fit before pitching
2. **Be a problem solver** — understand their challenges first
3. **Get to the truth** — a clear yes or no beats a polite maybe every time

---

## Mindset Shift

| Traditional | UTG |
|---|---|
| Lead with MyPass capabilities | Start with their challenges |
| Assume MyPass is the solution | Explore whether there's genuine alignment |
| Chase for responses | Respect their process and timeline |
| Counter objections | Validate concerns and reopen conversation |
| Defend MyPass features | Never defend — stay curious |

---

## Language Tone

**Always:** natural, conversational, calm, relaxed, gentle, respectful, humble, unassuming, authentic, attentive, friendly, helpful

**Never:** urgent, assumptive, pushy, defensive, salesy, feature-led

---

## Opening Patterns

**Instead of:** "I'd like to tell you about MyPass..."
**UTG:** "I'm just reaching out to see if you're open to different ideas about [specific challenge]"

**Instead of:** "MyPass can solve your workforce compliance issues..."
**UTG:** "I wanted to see if you might have any feedback about approaches to workforce visibility across multiple sites"

**Instead of:** "Let me show you how MyPass works..."
**UTG:** "I'm curious about your experience with contractor onboarding — are you open to discussing different ideas around that?"

## Objection Handling

### "We already have a vendor"
> "That's not a problem. I'm not looking to replace what you have. I'm just curious about your experience with [specific challenge area] — would you be open to sharing your perspective?"

### "We don't have budget"
> "That's not a problem. Budget aside, can you help me understand what [specific challenge] looks like in your operations?"

### "Send me information"
> "That's not a problem. Before I send anything specific, can you help me understand what particular challenge you're dealing with? That way I can share something more relevant."

### "We're not looking at new solutions right now"
> "That's completely understandable. I'm not trying to change your timeline. I'm just curious about [specific operational challenge] — are you open to sharing how you're currently handling that?"

## Templates by Scenario

### Cold Outreach
```
Hi [Name], just reaching out to see if you're open to different ideas about
[specific challenge]. I've been working with similar organisations on [problem area]
and I'm curious about your experience. Would you be open to a brief conversation about
what you're seeing? No agenda on my end — just genuinely interested in your perspective.
```

### Post-Discovery Follow-up
```
Thanks for sharing your insights about [specific challenge]. I found your perspective
on [their specific situation] really valuable. Based on what you shared, I thought you
might find [relevant resource] interesting, though no pressure to review it. If you'd
like to continue exploring [their challenge] at some point, I'm happy to share how
[similar organisation] approached a similar situation.
```

### When They Go Quiet
```
Hi [Name], I haven't heard back from you, which is completely fine. I know you're
busy with [their priorities]. I came across [relevant insight] and thought you might
find it interesting given what you shared about [their challenge]. No need to respond —
just thought it might be valuable. If you'd like to continue our conversation at
some point, just let me know.
```

### Budget / Commercial Discussion
```
I completely understand that budget is a consideration. That's not a problem at all.
I'm not trying to create a sales situation here. I'm just curious about [specific
challenge you discussed] — regardless of budget, how important is solving that
particular issue for your operations?
```

## Social Proof — UTG Framing

**Instead of:** "MyPass works with BHP, Rio Tinto, and Chevron"

**UTG:** "I've been working with organisations like BHP and Rio Tinto on contractor management challenges. I'm curious about your experience with [specific challenge] — how does that compare to what you're seeing?"

Proof points are conversation openers, not credentials to impress with.

---

## UTG Checklist — Run Before Every Prospect Email

- [ ] Problem-focused opening (not feature/capability lead)
- [ ] Pressure-free next step (optional, not demanded)
- [ ] Genuine question embedded (one is enough)
- [ ] No urgency language
- [ ] Permission to decline implied or explicit
- [ ] No assumptions about their problems or fit
- [ ] More questions than statements
- [ ] Prepared to validate concerns without defending
