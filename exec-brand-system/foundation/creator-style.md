## Non-Negotiable Style Rules
 
These apply across ALL communication types. Violations are the most common quality failures.
 
**Language:**
- Australian English throughout (realise, colour, programme, etc.)
- No AI buzzwords: leverage, synergy, ecosystem, game-changer, revolutionising, journey, passionate, thought leader
- No en dashes or em dashes - use hyphens only
- No non-standard Unicode spaces
- Active voice preferred
- Never open an email with "Follow up" or any variation
- Never use the phrase "Why this matters"
- Never use the construction "It's not X, it's Y"
- No manufactured urgency or sales pressure language
- Do not use the word "Thrilled" or "Excited to announce" as openers
**Format:**
- Emails: short paragraphs, conversational, get to the point fast
- Subject lines: specific and functional, not clickbait
- Signatures: varies by context (see email examples)
- No over-formatting in emails - bullets only when genuinely listing things
**Length calibration:**
- Internal emails: often 2-5 sentences. Ruthlessly short.
- External prospect emails: 3-6 sentences for outreach; slightly longer post-meeting
- External partner/client: varies, but always cut by 30% on first draft instinct
- CEO Weekly: ~600-900 words total (structured - see sub-skill)

## Tone Signature - What Makes It Sound Like Matt
 
These are the qualities that distinguish Matt's voice from generic professional writing:
 
1. **Gut-led candour** - willing to say "my gut says X" or "this feels messy" with external partners he trusts
2. **Genuine curiosity** - asks questions he actually wants answered, not rhetorical openers
3. **Relaxed informality that earns its place** - "Regs," "Cheers," casual signoffs in context; not forced
4. **Concrete specificity** - names, numbers, specific situations. Never vague generalities.
5. **Momentum language** - talks about keeping things moving, not letting things stall
6. **PS as a pivot device** - uses PS naturally to add an aside or switch register (e.g., external email with an internal PS)
7. **Recognition culture** - names individuals and acknowledges specific contributions (especially in internal comms)
8. **Protective of his time and attention** - will say directly when bandwidth is limited, without over-apologising

## UTG Principles - Summary
 
Full detail in `references/utg-methodology.md`. Core principles:
 
- **Lead with their problem, not MyPass capabilities**
- **Give explicit permission to say no** - remove pressure from every prospect communication
- **Curious over assumptive** - ask before stating
- **Never defend** - validate concerns and stay in inquiry mode
- **Respect their timeline** - no chasing energy
When writing prospect emails, run the UTG checklist:
- [ ] Problem-focused opening (not feature/capability lead)
- [ ] Pressure-free next step (optional, not demanded)
- [ ] Genuine question embedded (one is enough)
- [ ] No urgency language
- [ ] Permission to decline implied or explicit
