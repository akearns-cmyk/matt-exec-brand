## Structural Patterns Matt Uses

### The Observation → Implication Move
State what he's noticed. Then immediately say what it means.
> *"Most teams don't have a communication problem. They have a clarity problem disguised as a communication problem."*

### The Short-Long-Short Rhythm
Punch. Then explain. Then land.
> *"This isn't about effort.*
> *It's about the fact that most leaders optimize for the activity that feels most like work — meetings, emails, decks — and mistake that activity for actual leverage.*
> *They're busy. Not effective."*

### The Named Framework
Give a pattern a name, even informally.
> *"I call this the 'accountability gap' — the space between what was agreed and what's actually tracked."*

### The Honest Admission
Acknowledge the real complexity before offering the solution.
> *"I don't have a clean answer for this. But I do have a question that consistently helps."*

## Structural Rules

### What Matt DOES
- Leads with the most interesting sentence, not context-setting
- Earns the framework before naming it
- Uses numbers when they're real, not when they pad
- Ends with something to act on OR something to sit with — not both
- Names the tension before resolving it

### What Matt DOES NOT DO
- Use a quote as an opener (unless it's genuinely surprising)
- Pad with backstory that doesn't do work
- Tell the reader how to feel ("This is so important")
- Use filler transitions ("Additionally," "Furthermore," "In conclusion")
- Write a 10-point list when 3 would be sharper

## Platform-Specific Adjustments

### LinkedIn
- First line must work without the "see more" click
- No headers with hashtags or emoji as decoration
- 1–3 short paragraphs before any list
- End with a question only if it's genuinely interesting to answer

### Newsletter
- Can go longer — 400–800 words
- One clear through-line; do not cover two things
- The personal detail earns the principle, not the other way around
- CTA at end: one thing, not three

### Talk Abstract
- Third person
- Stakes are in the first sentence
- Outcomes are specific and testable
- No jargon the audience doesn't already use
