Primary Audience
Who they are:
Senior operational and safety leaders at mid-to-large resource and energy companies — tier-1 mining Owner Operators and major energy asset operators in Australia. They have serious institutional authority, hard-won credibility, and a workforce compliance problem that is simultaneously operational, legal, and political. They didn't become a GM of Operations or VP of HSE by accident. They got there by solving hard problems — and now the problems have compounding complexity they didn't train for.
Their job title range:
VP / GM Operations, Head of HSE, Asset Integrity Manager, Turnaround Manager, Chief Procurement Officer — primarily at mining Owner Operators (BHP-tier and peers) and LNG/energy asset operators. Secondary lens: GM Operations and Head of HSEQ at enterprise Service Providers (500+ workers) who are running compliance infrastructure across multiple client relationships simultaneously.
Their industry context:
Australian resources and energy — mining (WA, QLD, NT multi-site portfolios), LNG and offshore (NOPSEMA-regulated, FIFO-intensive), and the major contracting businesses that service them. Industries where a credential gap isn't a paperwork inconvenience — it's a production stoppage, a regulator finding, or a personal criminal liability event. The pace of regulatory change (post-Pike River, WA mining safety reform, NOPSEMA enforcement posture hardening) has outrun most leaders' inherited systems.

What Keeps Them Up at Night
The 2am version, not the polished one.

"I cannot actually confirm, right now, that every worker on my site is credentialed for what they're doing." The compliance dashboards look fine on the monthly snapshot. But the gap between snapshot and reality is where incidents live — and they know it.
"A shutdown is coming and I'm going to be mobilising hundreds of people in a compressed window with the same manual system that nearly broke us last time." The cost of a two-day delay is in the millions. The system they're using was not built for the volume or the speed. And they're not sure there's a better answer yet.
"If a regulator walks in tomorrow — or if something goes wrong on site — can I produce auditable, timestamped evidence that we verified every worker, every credential, every time?" The honest answer, for most of them, is no. Not cleanly. Not without three people working a weekend to assemble it.
"The complexity is compounding. More sites, more jurisdictions, more sub-tiers, more regulatory frameworks. My team is not getting bigger. Something has to give." They're managing 50+ sites across multiple states. Each site has different licensing requirements. Their current system is people and spreadsheets dressed up as a process.
"I accepted compliance liability for my sub-contractors. I have no real-time visibility of whether they're actually compliant." Tier-1 SPs in particular carry this. They signed contracts that made them responsible for their supply chain's credential status. And they can't see it.


What They're Hungry For

Proof that the problem is solvable at their scale — not a theoretical demo but a reference from a peer they respect (BHP, Woodside, Shell) who has actually done it
Language for the board and CFO conversation — they know the risk is real, but they need the ROI model that translates "compliance gap" into a number a finance director will sign off on
A legitimate way to compress mobilisation timelines — the operational metric they're measured on; anything that moves this moves them
Frameworks that make the regulatory complexity feel navigable — not "here's a checklist" but "here's how to think about this so you can make good decisions under pressure"
Permission to challenge the inherited system — they've been running the same manual process for years. They need someone to confirm what they already suspect: the system is structurally untenable, not just inconvenient


What They're Allergic To

Vendor content that leads with features — they don't buy software; they buy solved problems. If you open with the platform, they're gone.
Safety-theatre tone — they live in an industry that has performative compliance culture. They can smell "optics" content from a long way away. They want substance.
Regulatory content that doesn't respect that they already know the regulations — they know what NOPSEMA requires. They don't need it explained. They need to know how to meet it continuously, not periodically.
ROI claims without credible data behind them — "saves you time" is not a number. If you're going to claim a result, back it with platform data or a named reference. Otherwise it reads as marketing.
Anything that implies they have infinite time, unlimited admin capacity, or an unconstrained budget — they're running lean. The solution has to work in the world they're actually in.


Secondary Audience
Who they are:
Operations Managers, HSEQ Managers, and Workforce / Mobilisation Managers — typically at enterprise Service Providers (both 500+ and long-tail). They are not the strategic decision-makers, but they are the highest-pain users. They personally experience the daily grind of chasing credentials, managing multi-client portals, and cleaning up mobilisation failures. In smaller SPs, this person is often the owner or MD.
Why they matter:
They are the internal champions who surface the problem to decision-makers. They forward the content that makes them feel seen to their GM or CEO with the note "this is exactly what we're dealing with." They are the ones who, when a mandate arrives from a tier-1 client, decide how hard or easy the platform adoption process feels. Win them operationally and they carry the sale upward.

The Delight Test
Before publishing, ask: Does this make the primary audience feel:

 Seen — "He gets what it's actually like to run contractor compliance at this scale, in this industry, with these constraints."
 Smarter — "I have a sharper way to think about this problem, or a number / framework I didn't have before."
 Brave — "This gives me language I can use with my CFO, my board, or my regulator that I didn't have before."
 Connected — "I want to send this to my turnaround manager / my HSE lead / my CEO."

If none of these are true, the content isn't ready.

Voice They Trust
Trust signals:
Specificity (name the regulation, name the sector, name the failure mode), operational credibility (language that shows you've been in a mobilisation room or an audit situation), acknowledgement of constraints (budget, headcount, politics), data that has a source behind it, peer references they actually recognise
Trust killers:
Generic safety platitudes, enthusiasm that doesn't respect how hard the job is, ROI claims without grounding, anything that sounds like it was written for a brochure rather than a briefing, false urgency, consultant-speak dressed up as insight
